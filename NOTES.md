# Notes

## Why this line

The dread comes from a chair — the most ordinary object in a kitchen. Nothing is broken, nothing jumps out. On day one the detail is almost too small to mention: the chair is an inch away from where it was left. It could be memory, carelessness, anything.

## How the two-day dread builds

Day one plants uncertainty. Day two resolves it in the worst direction: the chair is not just moved, it is tucked in neatly. That neatness is the horror — it implies intention, patience, and presence. The closing image, of someone who has "finally sat down to wait," reframes both days: whatever moved the chair was not passing through, it was settling in, and it is still waiting.

The escalation is quiet and domestic rather than violent, so the unease lingers after the sentence ends instead of discharging in a scare.
